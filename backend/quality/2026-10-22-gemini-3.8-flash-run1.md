**S1 Mathematics: Advanced Percentages in Real-Life Contexts**

1. (stretch, Calculate the original value before a percentage discount) A shopkeeper in Owino Market reduced the price of a school bag by 20% during a promotion. A parent paid 24,000 shillings for the bag. What was the original price of the bag before the discount?
    - **30,000 shillings** ✓
    - 28,800 shillings
    - 19,200 shillings
    - 32,000 shillings
    - _Explanation: Since 20% was deducted, 24,000 shillings represents 80% of the original price. Dividing 24,000 by 0.8 gives the original price of 30,000 shillings._
2. (stretch, Solve multi-step problems involving percentages of remaining quantities) Farmer Okello harvested 500 bags of maize. He sold 40% of the harvest to a local secondary school and then stored 30% of the remaining bags for his family. How many bags of maize did he have left to sell at the market?
    - **210 bags** ✓
    - 150 bags
    - 200 bags
    - 290 bags
    - _Explanation: After selling 40% (200 bags), 300 bags remained. He then stored 30% of 300 (90 bags), leaving 300 minus 90, which equals 210 bags._
3. (stretch, Calculate percentage profit including additional expenses) A fruit vendor bought a crate of 120 mangoes for 32,000 shillings and spent 8,000 shillings on transport to the market. She sold all the mangoes at 400 shillings each. What was her percentage profit based on her total expenses?
    - **20%** ✓
    - 25%
    - 16.7%
    - 50%
    - _Explanation: Her total cost was 40,000 shillings (32,000 + 8,000) and her total sales were 48,000 shillings (120 × 400). The profit was 8,000 shillings, which is (8,000 ÷ 40,000) × 100% = 20%._
4. (stretch, Analyze successive percentage increases and decreases) In January, the price of a tin of beans was 10,000 shillings. In February, the price increased by 10%. In March, the shop reduced the February price by 10%. What was the final price of the tin of beans in March?
    - **9,900 shillings** ✓
    - 10,000 shillings
    - 9,000 shillings
    - 10,100 shillings
    - _Explanation: In February, the price rose to 11,000 shillings (10,000 + 1,000). The 10% decrease in March is calculated on 11,000 (1,100 shillings off), giving a final price of 9,900 shillings._

Parent note: Your child is practising solving real-life percentage problems involving discounts, profits, and price changes. You can help by asking them to calculate discounts or price increases when you shop together at the local market.
